I study what happens when people and machines think together. Generative AI has moved into creative and professional work faster than our understanding of it, and the interesting questions are not about what the models can do but about what changes in us: how a group generates ideas differently with a language model in the room, how politeness and trust shift when the partner is a machine, how professionals defend their sense of craft when AI reaches into it. That work appears at CHI, CSCW, IUI, and ICIS, and I chair the ACM IUI Steering Committee.

Underneath it sits a longer preoccupation with the traces people leave in data, and with the traces they don't. I began with online reviews, showing that the emotions a viewer felt while watching a film can be recovered from the review they wrote afterwards, and that those emotional signatures can diversify recommendations and explain why some reviews are useful to other readers. Following that question led somewhere less obvious. Most machine learning hunts for the patterns present in data; I became more interested in which *expected* signals are absent, and what their absence reveals. Learning via Surprisability rests on the idea that a missing feature can be as informative as a present one. It attributes authorship, flags impersonation, and surfaces interpretable anomalies in high-dimensional data, without labels. It has since become a working method across my group, including as the analytical lens in our studies of human and AI co-creation.

A third strand concerns timing. Real interactions have rhythm: people meet at particular moments, for particular durations, and treating a network as a static structure discards most of what matters. My group showed that these temporal dynamics govern how an airborne disease moves through a community more strongly than the transmissibility of the pathogen itself. I gave the keynote on this work at NetSciX'25 and presented it more recently at SigmaPhi, and we released the first open-source temporal path-preserving random network generator alongside it.

I chair the [ACM IUI Steering Committee](https://iui.acm.org/steering/) and serve as an Associate Editor at *ACM Transactions on Interactive Intelligent Systems*. I hold a Ph.D. in Computer Science from the Hebrew University of Jerusalem, and an M.Sc. and B.Sc. from the Technion. Prospective students: please see the [lab page](/people/) and get in touch.
